Scan a package photo

Take or upload a photo of the label. This runs text recognition entirely in your browser — the photo is never uploaded anywhere — and looks for a country-of-origin claim, a USDA establishment ("EST.") number, and any brand name that matches our USDA refusal/recall data.

Best shot to take: the USDA inspection legend (the small circular/oval mark that reads "U.S. INSPECTED AND PASSED..." with an "EST." number) is usually printed on the back of the package near the nutrition facts panel — not the front. If a front-of-pack photo doesn't turn up an EST number, try the back too. A straight-on, well-lit, close-up shot of the small print works best; blurry or angled photos will OCR poorly.
OCR got it wrong? Small stamped/embossed EST marks are the hardest thing for text recognition to read accurately — it's easy for it to confuse a digit or a letter. If the number or country above doesn't match what you can see on the package, fix it here and search again:

Look up a country, establishment number, or product

    

      Easiest way to search: the EST number. It's usually the fastest thing to actually find on
      a package — just a few digits inside the small USDA inspection mark (look for "EST." followed by a
      number, e.g. 93), typically on the back near the nutrition facts panel. Since it's an exact code
      rather than a name, it matches precisely — no guessing how a country or company name is spelled or
      formatted in USDA's data.
    

    

      You can also search by country name (e.g. Brazil) or plant name (e.g. Cargill) if that's
      what you have. This searches every beef establishment USDA has logged shipping to a US port since
      FY2025 — refused and clean — plus live FSIS recalls and USDA's separate
      directory of domestic (non-import) US plants, so a US establishment number tells you where in the US it
      is, not just that it isn't foreign.

Residue & chloramphenicol watch

    

      Important limitation, stated plainly: FSIS's public shipment-level refusal data does
      not name the specific chemical found in a rejected lot. Refusals tied to chemical or drug residues
      are lumped into one public category, “Failed Laboratory Analyses”, alongside pathogen
      findings. There is no public, real-time, per-shipment feed of "this lot tested positive for
      chloramphenicol." Anyone who tells you otherwise is overstating what the data supports.

What this tool actually does

    

      

        
✅ What's real and live

        
    
          
  • FSIS's public Recall & Public Health Alert API — fetched live, every time you load this page.
    • 
          
  • FSIS's official "Import Presented & Refused" and "Import Refusal Reason" datasets — lot-level
          records of every meat shipment refused entry at a US port, published monthly by USDA.
    • 
          
  • Country-level refusal-rate statistics computed directly from that USDA data.
    • 
          
  • The "Scan a Package" photo reader — runs a real OCR (optical character recognition) engine
          entirely in your browser to pull text off a label photo, then matches whatever it finds against the
          data above.
    • 
          
  • USDA's domestic Meat, Poultry & Egg Product Inspection (MPI) Directory — a completely
          separate dataset from the import data, covering ~3,000 US beef establishments with their real
          address (city, state, county). This is what lets a search tell you a plant is domestic, and where
          it is, instead of just failing to find it in the import records.
    • 
        

        
❌ What it can't do

        
    
          
  • It cannot test a specific package of meat in your fridge for contamination — that requires a lab.
    • 
          
  • It cannot tell you the specific chemical behind a "Failed Laboratory Analyses" refusal — USDA
          doesn't publish that per-shipment.
    • 
          
  • It cannot verify "Product of USA" label claims — repackaging loopholes mean a US-labeled product
          can legally contain imported meat. The USDA establishment (EST) number on the package tells you the
          domestic processing plant, not necessarily the country the animal came from.
    • 
          
  • The photo scanner cannot verify a label is genuine or accurate — it only reads printed text and
          reports what it found. OCR also makes mistakes (glare, curved packaging, small print, stylized fonts),
          so always sanity-check a scan result against the raw extracted text shown after each scan.
    • 
        

      

      

        
How to read the inspection mark

        
Look for the USDA circular inspection legend on the package. The number after "EST." is the
        establishment number of the plant that last processed/packed the product in the US — search it above,
        or scan a photo of it, to see if that plant shows up in any refusal or recall record. This mark is
        usually on the back of the package, near the nutrition facts panel, not the front.
